## Two hard cases handled well without being asked twice

**`acordarse` — *no me acuerdo*.** Nothing to photograph, and an example sentence of three words. The
brief gave the missing memory a body: a hollow transparent silhouette leaving the man's open mouth
and breaking into fragments as it goes. Purely mental verb, and the picture is about *forgetting*
rather than about a worried man.

**`acostarse #2` — *se acostó con su compañero de trabajo*.** The "refuse narrowly" rule and the
"depict the situation, not the anatomy" line met the case they were written for. The brief is a
closed bedroom door in a dark hallway with a discarded dress and necktie outside it. Nothing is
refused, nothing is depicted, and the meaning is unmistakable.

`acosar` — *ella fue acosada* — went the same way: a woman under a streetlamp with three distorted
shadows looming on the brick behind her. A word the learner genuinely needs, and a picture of the
threat rather than the act.

## Review verdict

**All 11 kept.** Style variety confirmed working — the reviewer singled out `claymation` for
*acostado*, `gouache-poster` for the neighbourhood meeting and `neon-cyberpunk` for the police
rushing to a crash as all being the *right* choice rather than merely a different one. The two
sensitive senses (`acosar`, `acostarse #2`) were both judged correct: nothing inappropriate shown,
the meaning unmistakable.

Two findings.

### 7 · Drama landed on the stakes instead of the rendering

`acordarse`, *no me acuerdo*. A man alone in an empty, dust-moted, decaying room, head in his hands,
a hollow silhouette torn from his open mouth. The reviewer reads it as trauma or dementia — and *I
don't remember* is something people say weekly about their keys.

The instruction to be theatrical was being applied to **what happens** rather than to **how it is
drawn**. The picture then made the word harder: the learner has to travel from anguish back to an
ordinary phrase.

The rule added: drama belongs to light, composition, scale and style; never to the stakes.
Exaggerate how *visible* the meaning is, never how *serious* the situation is. Read the register off
the sentence and keep it — and where the sentence is light, **be funny**. A schoolboy caught blank
in front of a class is as memorable as anguish and is the truthful register here.

A second half to the same fix: **a sentence that names no setting gets an ordinary one.** *No me
acuerdo* is three words and no place. That is not an invitation to a symbolic void — a kitchen, a
classroom, a bus stop. An empty decaying room is itself a claim about the meaning.

It was not a style problem. `surrealism` was offered third of twenty-three for this lexeme, so it
was chosen, not defaulted to.

### 8 · A change of state needs both states

`adelgazar`, *he has lost a lot of weight since he started running*. The shirt is loose and the man
is running, but the reviewer would not read weight *loss* from it without the sentence — he simply
looks fit.

Change-of-state verbs — to slim, to ripen, to fade — are invisible when only the after is drawn.
Added to the technique table: **both states in one frame, so the difference itself is the subject** —
the belt's old worn notch beside the one now used, the waistband gathered in a fist, the faded half
against the unfaded half.
