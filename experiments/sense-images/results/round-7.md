# Round 7 · 2026-09-06

84 images, the first drawn with **sampled style hints**: each style is presented with three of its
eight-or-so example subjects, drawn per lexeme, so it reads as an association rather than a rule.
**83 of 84 kept.**

## Did the hints hurt?

No, and the numbers point the other way, though not yet far enough to be sure:

| | styles used | top two |
|---|---:|---|
| no hints (n=502) | 21 | 45% · gouache-poster, comic-book |
| sampled hints (n=84) | 18 | **36%** · comic-book, oil-painting |

84 is too small to lean on. The useful verdict is the reviewer's, because the failure mode of the
round-5 hints was never the distribution — it was pictures that made you ask *why that style?*
Round 7: *"except for art nouveau I haven't seen any bad usages... they were more opportunistic, a
little more risky."* `art-nouveau` and `papercraft-diorama`, both effectively unused before, arrived
and worked.

So the mechanism is fixed. A fixed sentence per style is a rule the writer applies; three shifting
examples are a reminder of what a style can do.

**Both modes are keepers**, which is the finding that matters for the product. Round 6 without hints
was *"on point, and not uniform"*; round 7 with them is less predictable and equally apt. That is a
matter of taste, so it becomes a setting — see `docs/acervo-sense-images.md` §05.

## 16 · A bystander outranks the subject

The one rejection. **`imprescindible`** — indispensable — at an airport checkpoint: a flustered
traveller with a heap of documents, a second sailing through on one passport, and a **border officer**
with a raised palm.

The brief is not badly written. It says in so many words that the passport is *"brightly lit and
unmistakable"*. It is not, in the picture: the officer is, because he is standing, central, and in
charge. A viewer looks at people before objects and at whoever holds the power before anyone else,
so a character added to make the *situation* work quietly takes the picture.

Added: keep the cast small — two people is usually plenty, one is often better — and when the subject
is an object, give it the best-lit place and have whoever is present hold it or look at it. Anyone
whose only job is to make the situation legible belongs small, at the edge, or cut.

This is the third variant of one recurring problem, after the pink erasers and the bus tickets:
**detail added for plausibility competes with the subject.** People are the most expensive kind.

## 17 · The style must not be doing the explaining

The reviewer's observation, and it generalises further than the case that prompted it: *"the style
usually doesn't convey the idea at all — it may set the mood."* A woodblock mountain for
*insurmountable* looked apt, but the aptness was the style's, not the scene's.

Added as a test the writer can apply: imagine the brief rendered in a completely different style. If
the word stops being visible, the scene was never demonstrating it and the style was standing in.

## `art-nouveau`, adjusted rather than removed

Its two appearances were the weakest style choices in the batch — a headache *fading*, and a singer
*enchanting* an audience. Its hint list was the cause: "a person absorbed in something beautiful",
"someone dressed for an evening out" match any scene with an appealing person in it. Rewritten
toward meanings rather than prettiness — vanity, temptation, ceremony, *beauty used as an argument* —
and its description now says the ornament frames the subject rather than filling the frame, which
was the reviewer's second objection.

## Noted, not acted on

- *Unusual heat for that time of year* produced a plausible hot Christmas, but nothing in it is
  visibly **atypical**. Covered by the existing accent rule; not a new mechanism.
- *Swollen* was exaggerated to the point of being unsettling. Accepted as within tolerance.
- Several entries were judged limited by the **article** rather than the picture — a weak example
  sentence, a definition that does not explain the word. That is the capture prompt's problem, and
  it is a good sign: the constraint has moved off this stage.
