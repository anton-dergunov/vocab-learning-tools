🧠 Why Stories Work for Vocabulary Learning
	1.	Contextual Anchoring — Words appear in meaningful, emotionally engaging situations. This creates long-term memory traces far stronger than rote memorization.
Example: learning “deprimido” (depressed) in a story where a character misses home will link the word to emotions.
	2.	Repetition without boredom — You can weave the same word multiple times naturally.
“Ella estaba deprimida. Tan deprimida que ni el sol parecía alegrarla…”
	3.	Narrative predictability — The human brain is wired to follow stories. Even unfamiliar words are guessed through narrative cues.
	4.	Emotional charge — Funny, shocking, or touching events increase dopamine release — boosting memory consolidation.
	5.	Multi-word learning — A story naturally exposes collocations and idioms (e.g., “ponerse triste”, “dar miedo”).

⸻

🧩 How You Could Use Stories in Your System
	1.	Word Sampling
	•	Randomly sample 10–15 words from your vocabulary set.
	•	Optionally: bias towards words from the same Topic (e.g., “Emotions”, “Health”).
	2.	Story Generator
	•	Create a short, engaging story (100–300 words) that naturally uses these words multiple times.
	•	Style options:
	•	🕵️ Mystery
	•	❤️ Romance
	•	😂 Comedy
	•	🌍 Travel
	•	👻 Supernatural
	3.	Learning Exercises
	•	Fill-in-the-blank: remove the target words from the story.
	•	Translation challenge: provide key sentences and ask to translate.
	•	Story retelling: learner retells the story using the new words.
	•	Mini comprehension quiz.
	4.	Spaced review: Later, the same words reappear in new, different stories.
